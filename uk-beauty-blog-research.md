# UK beauty blog research

Research date: 18 November 2026

## Ten reference sites

1. **Live That Glow** - https://www.livethatglow.com/
   Editorial lessons: curiosity-led headlines, named writers and experts, clear testing windows, and careful separation of immediate cosmetic effects from longer-term claims. Product reviews move from personal context to ingredient explanation, individual reviews, recommendation and where to buy.

2. **A Woman's Confidence** - https://awomansconfidence.com/
   Editorial lessons: the writer's skin type and concerns appear early; sponsorship and affiliate disclosures are prominent; claims, application, texture, results and verdict are clearly separated. The review also explains the limitations of before-and-after photography.

3. **British Beauty Blogger** - https://britishbeautyblogger.com/
   Editorial lessons: concise first-person reviews, direct sensory descriptions and visible labels such as unpaid, sample and affiliate. The price and retailer arrive after the useful opinion rather than dominating the opening.

4. **Caroline Hirons** - https://www.carolinehirons.com/blogs/caroline-hirons
   Editorial lessons: a searchable educational archive, practical cheat sheets, numbered routines and short definitions for technical terms such as humectant and occlusive. Author credentials and related reading support trust and discovery.

5. **Really Ree** - https://reallyree.com/
   Editorial lessons: prominent updated dates, distinct trending and latest areas, visual product evidence, and seasonal content that is maintained rather than published once and forgotten.

6. **The Beauty Shortlist** - https://www.thebeautyshortlist.com/
   Editorial lessons: a clear natural-beauty niche, seasonal edits, expert judging panels and transparent category structures. Its strongest lesson is to give curation a recognisable editorial point of view.

7. **Cult Beauty Blog** - https://www.cultbeauty.co.uk/blog/
   Editorial lessons: useful content groups such as Tried and Tested, Ask the Experts and Ingredient Focus; named editors; concise article descriptions; and a close but legible relationship between educational content and shopping.

8. **Makeup Savvy** - https://www.makeupsavvy.co.uk/
   Editorial lessons: accessible seasonal transitions, budget-conscious product choices, first-person routine context and explicit advertising labels.

9. **A Beauty Junkie in London** - https://www.beautyjunkielondon.com/category/beauty
   Editorial lessons: personal mistakes become useful reader advice, seasonal wellness broadens the beauty remit, and testing diaries create a reason to follow later updates.

10. **Science & Skincare** - https://scienceandskincare.blog/
    Editorial lessons: educational clusters around barrier health, FAQs, a skin glossary, skin conditions and quizzes. Complex topics are turned into repeatable reader-facing explainers.

## Template used for Decode K-Beauty

- Blog index: a quiet editorial heading, one featured seasonal guide with a single image, and a simple list of additional stories without promotional modules.
- Article opening: specific headline, practical deck, publication date, reading time, quick answer and table of contents.
- Context: describe the reader's concern before introducing products; distinguish desk research from first-hand testing.
- Explanation: define the relevant skin or ingredient concepts and link authoritative sources.
- Routine: numbered, concrete steps with morning and evening placement.
- Product edit: explain the role, the suitable reader and a reason to skip each product.
- Decision support: skin-type adjustments, common mistakes and FAQs.
- Close: a restrained verdict, sources, research method and affiliate disclosure.

The implementation borrows structural ideas, not copy, photography, branding or invented testing claims.
